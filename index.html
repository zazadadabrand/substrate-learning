Material 001 · Visual Language · Atom 01
Typeface Classification
The atom: classify any typeface into its family on sight. Start with the lesson, it assumes you know nothing and builds the eye concept by concept. The reference is your lookup sheet afterward. The drill decides whether you know it.
Step 1

The five tells

Every classification decision runs through the same five features. You never memorize typefaces one by one, you read these features and the class falls out.

Stroke contrastHow much thick and thin varies within a letter. None in most sans, extreme in didones.
Stress axisThe angle of the thin parts. Tilted means older and humanist, vertical means rational and modern.
Serif shapeBracketed and soft, thin and unbracketed, or heavy rectangular slabs. Or absent entirely.
ApertureHow open letters like a, c, and e are. Humanist forms open wide, grotesques close up.
TerminalsHow strokes end: cut flat, ball shaped, or flared. The g and a carry the most information.

The six classes

Humanist sans
Canon: Gill Sans, Frutiger, Optima
Sans serif built on calligraphic bones. Open apertures, slight stroke modulation, warmth. If a sans looks like it could have been written by a hand, it is humanist.
Aa RgeAa Rge
Grotesque
Canon: Helvetica, Univers, Akzidenz Grotesk, Franklin Gothic
The neutral workhorses. Uniform strokes, closed apertures, flat horizontal terminals, no written memory.
Aa RgeAa Rge
Geometric sans
Canon: Futura, Avant Garde, DIN
Letters drawn from circles and lines. Compass circle o, no contrast, constructed rather than written.
Aa RgeAa Rge
Transitional serif
Canon: Baskerville, Times, Georgia
The middle of serif history. Moderate contrast, near vertical stress, bracketed serifs. The voice of books.
Aa RgeAa Rge
Didone
Canon: Bodoni, Didot
Extreme contrast, vertical stress, hairline unbracketed serifs, ball terminals. The fashion masthead class.
Aa RgeAa Rge
Slab serif
Canon: Rockwell, Clarendon, Courier
Serifs as heavy rectangular blocks, low contrast, sturdy and mechanical. Serifs that could hold weight.
Specimens are rendered in freely licensed proxy faces from each class, since the canonical faces are not web licensed. The tells transfer exactly: you are learning features, not fonts.

Material 005 · Visual Language · Atom 05
Color Harmonies
The atom: name the six color harmonies and build a palette in each. The lesson teaches the wheel as a map and each harmony as a shape drawn on it. The drill shows you palettes and you call the harmony, cold.
Step 1

The wheel and the six shapes

Every harmony is a geometric shape drawn on the color wheel. Learn the shape and you can both recognize a harmony on sight and construct one from any starting hue. The offsets below are in degrees around the wheel.

Palettes on this page are generated live from the formulas, so every visit shows fresh examples of the same six shapes. That is the point: you are learning geometry, not specific color combinations.
